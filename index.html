Body image & self-worth

You are more than how you look.

Society, social media, trends, and other people's opinions can shape how we see ourselves. This space invites you to question those messages—not to change your body, but to treat yourself with more respect and freedom.

A reminder

Your worth was never meant to be measured by how closely you match someone else's idea of beauty.

🪞 Notice the messages

Beauty standards change across time and cultures. Popularity does not make a standard a rule you must follow.

Ask yourself: Whose voice am I hearing when I criticise how I look?

🌱 Move toward body respect

You do not have to love every part of your appearance every day. You can begin with neutrality: your body deserves care, comfort, and respect.

Try: “I can be kind to myself even when I feel uncomfortable about how I look.”

📱 Curate what you see

Notice which accounts or comments leave you feeling judged or less-than. You can mute, unfollow, take a break, or choose content that supports a wider and kinder view of people.

✍️ Reflect gently

There is no right answer. You can reflect privately without sharing it.

Who taught me what a “good” or “acceptable” appearance is? Which of those messages do I actually agree with?

Inner Light will not rate bodies, compare users, or tell anyone they need to change their appearance. If worries about appearance are affecting daily life, talking with a trusted person or qualified counsellor can help.

Calm & Care

Pause. Breathe. Ground.

Gentle tools for anxious, tense or overwhelming moments.

🌬️ One-minute breathing

Breathe in gently for 4 seconds, then out slowly for 6 seconds. Keep it comfortable; don't force your breath.

Ready

🧭 Grounding: 5–4–3–2–1

Gently notice 5 things you see, 4 things you feel, 3 things you hear, 2 things you smell, and 1 thing you taste or imagine tasting.

🫶 When emotions feel strong

Sit somewhere comfortable, relax your shoulders, breathe naturally, and choose one small next step. Contact someone you trust if you need support.

These are general calming tools, not emergency treatment. If you feel unsafe, seek immediate help from a trusted person nearby or local emergency services.
